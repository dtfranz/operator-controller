    Design: ClusterObjectDeployment / ClusterObjectSet referenced-secret ownership

    Date: 2026-10-24 Status: Draft (pending approval) Branch: cod-cos-secret-ownership (off main)

    Intent and why

    ClusterObjectSet (COS) phases can reference serialized object manifests stored in Secrets (ObjectSourceRef{name, namespace, key}), and OLM packs rendered bundle content into immutable, content-addressed Secrets of type olm.operatorframework.io/object-data. We need a clear, self-contained rule for who owns those Secrets and when they get deleted, so that content Secrets do not leak across upgrades, revision churn, or deletion — while keeping the ClusterObjectDeployment (COD) and COS usable on their own, outside any ClusterExtension (CE).

    Why now / the motivating problem: on the operator-controller-use-cod branch, ownership of packed Secrets was moved onto the ClusterExtension, which is long-lived and never drops ownership. A code review found that this leaks Secrets: once content changes across an upgrade, the old content's Secrets are never referenced by a live revision again and are never deleted until the CE itself is removed. We want an ownership model whose lifetime is scoped to the things that actually consume the content (the COS revisions), not to the CE.

    Scope decision (set by the human): this design covers only the object-controller side — COD and COS. The CE / operator-controller side (who creates the Secrets and how it bridges the pre-COS window) is deferred and captured here only as a contract the creator must honor.

    Goals

    • A referenced Secret's lifecycle follows the COS(es) that reference it: it is deleted once no live COS references it.
    • No leaks across revision churn, COD deletion, or COS pruning.
    • COD and COS are usable standalone (outside a CE); cleanup must not depend on any CE-side controller.
    • Handle adoption: a COS created before the COD that later manages it (same spec.group), with a matching or a new template.
    • Claim (adopt) every referenced Secret, regardless of type, so its deletion follows the COS. The object-data-only restriction is deferred to future work.

    Non-goals

    • Who creates the packed Secrets, and closing the window before any COS exists. That is the creator's responsibility (CE / operator-controller), deferred. See The creator contract.
    • Changing the packing format, content-addressed naming, or immutability of the Secrets.
    • Changing how the COS validates or reports on referenced content (the existing Ready=False/PreconditionsNotMet behavior stays as-is).
    • CE-side ownership of Secrets (being removed; out of scope here).

    Anti-goals (what would make this a failure)

    • Adopting or deleting a Secret the COS does not reference. (Deleting a Secret the COS does reference is expected — that is the GC semantics; narrowing which Secrets may be referenced is deferred to future work.)
    • A design that only avoids leaks while a CE exists (i.e. not standalone-safe).
    • Reintroducing a long-lived owner (CE or COD) that must actively remove its ownerRefs to avoid leaking — fragile bookkeeping we explicitly reject.
    • Premature deletion of a Secret a live (or just-created / just-adopted) COS still needs.

    Core principle: own, not manage

    The object-controller (COD/COS) owns the Secrets a COS references strictly as a garbage-collection anchor — so a Secret's deletion follows the COS(es) that reference it — and does not manage them in any other sense.

    • Own = add a single, non-controller, additive, idempotent ownerRef. The COS becomes a thing whose disappearance reclaims the Secret. That one metadata write is the entire extent of what the object-controller does to a Secret.
    • Not manage = it is not the controller of the Secret; it does not watch it, does not mutate or repair its content (they are immutable; it only reads them via resolveObjectRef), and takes no responsibility for its existence or validity. A missing/invalid Secret is reported by the COS (Ready=False/PreconditionsNotMet), never created or fixed by it.

    Controller=false is a direct consequence of this principle, not an incidental choice: the controller flag confers management/stewardship semantics (single controller-of-record, .Owns() watch wiring, adoption/collision arbitration) that we explicitly do not want — and that would forbid the multi-owner model (shared content referenced by several COSes). We want only the GC cascade, which works for non-controller owners.

    Everything the object-controller deliberately omits — a sweeper, a finalizer, COD Secret ownership, declarative ownerRef removal — would be managing. The responsibility to manage a Secret (create it, bridge the pre-COS window, steward its content) stays with its creator (see the creator contract).

    Key facts this design relies on

    1. Kubernetes GC deletes a dependent only when all its owners are gone. As each owner is deleted, GC strips that ownerRef; when the last referenced owner is gone, the dependent is deleted. Multiple owners therefore means "deleted when the last one goes."
    2. Manually removing an ownerRef does not delete the dependent. A dependent left with zero ownerRefs is a normal orphan and is never GC'd. Deletion only happens via owner deletion (cascade) or an explicit Delete. (This is why an owner cannot "release" a Secret by un-referencing it — it must either be deleted itself or the Secret must be explicitly deleted.)
    3. At most one Controller=true ownerRef is allowed per object. A Secret may have several non-controller owners.
    4. OwnerRefs on an immutable Secret are allowed. Immutability locks data, not metadata; main already patches ownerRefs onto immutable Secrets.
    5. Cross-scope ownerRefs are valid for a namespaced dependent owned by a cluster-scoped owner (COS/COD are cluster-scoped; Secrets are namespaced).
    6. Secret metadata is outside both identity hashes. The COD template hash is DeepHashObject(spec.template) — it covers the refs (name/namespace/key), not the Secret object. The content-addressed Secret name hashes data only. So ownerRef changes never churn a revision or rename a Secret. (Content changes do flow into identity, indirectly, via the content-addressed name in the ref.)

    The model: COS-only ownership

    Each COS owns the Secrets its phases reference, via a non-controller ownerRef, regardless of type. That is the entire object-controller mechanism. It is sufficient because:

    • Revision churn: a new template produces a new COS that owns the new Secrets; the old COS is archived and pruned by the COD, and GC cascades its Secrets. ✔
    • COD deletion: the COD controller-owns its COSes, so deleting the COD cascades to the COSes, whose Secrets then cascade. ✔
    • Standalone COS: owns its referenced Secrets and cascades them on its own deletion — no COD required. ✔
    • Shared content: two revisions with identical content reference the same content-addressed Secret; each COS adds a non-controller ownerRef; the Secret survives until the last referencing COS is gone. ✔ (This is why ownerRefs must be non-controller.)

    Everything that was ever referenced by a COS is reclaimed by COS ownership. Everything that was never referenced by a COS is, by definition, outside the object-controller's knowledge and is the creator's responsibility (below).

    What the COD does (nothing new for Secrets)

    The COD does not own Secrets, does not run a sweeper, and needs no finalizer for Secrets. Its existing responsibilities are unchanged: it owns its COSes and prunes archived revisions, and pruning cascades Secret cleanup through COS ownership.

    The COD also does not gain a PreconditionsNotMet check for referenced Secrets. A missing/invalid referenced Secret does not block COS creation; the COS reports Ready=False/PreconditionsNotMet at resolve time and recovers when the Secret appears. The COD keeps its existing validateTemplateMetadata check, which guards a different failure mode (invalid template metadata makes the COS Create itself fail at the API server). Optionally, the COD may propagate the latest revision's Ready reason/message onto its own status so a stuck precondition is legible — surfacing, not re-validating.

    Any referenced Secret is adopted and garbage-collected

    The ref contract stays as it is today — a COS phase may reference a Secret of any type (the existing immutability precondition still applies). This design does not tighten that; see Future work.

    What it adds is uniform GC semantics: every referenced Secret is adopted (owned) by the referencing COS and garbage-collected when that COS is deleted, regardless of type. The claim is not type-gated.

    Known consequence: because any referenced Secret is adopted, referencing a Secret hands its lifecycle to the COS — including a user-supplied Secret a bare COS points at. That is accepted for now. The future object-data-only restriction (below) is what will eventually prevent a COS from referencing (and thus adopting) a Secret that was never meant to be OLM-managed. Until then, "referencing a Secret means the COS owns and will delete it" is the documented contract.

    Future work

    • Restrict referenceable Secret types to object-data. Today a COS phase may reference a Secret of any type, and this design adopts+GCs whatever is referenced. A later change should make a referenced Secret that is not olm.operatorframework.io/object-data a precondition failure (Ready=False/PreconditionsNotMet), so a COS can only reference (and therefore adopt/delete) Secrets that were meant to be OLM-managed content. This removes the "referencing a user's ordinary Secret deletes it" footgun by construction. It is a tightening of the (experimental) ref contract and is deferred so this change can land the GC semantics first.
    • The CE-side creator contract (below) and the applier Controller=true → non-controller-bridge conversion.

    The creator contract (deferred, CE side)

    A referenced Secret's lifecycle transfers to a COS once that COS adds its ownerRef. Until a COS owns it, the Secret's creator is responsible for it. A controller that uses COD/COS to manage cluster content should add a bridging ownerRef to the Secret at creation time and remove it once it observes that a COS has taken ownership.

    This closes the two windows the object-controller cannot see:

    • Creation window: Secret created before any COS exists → the creator's bridge owns it, so it cascades if the creator is deleted.
    • Claim gap: a COS exists and references the Secret but has not yet patched its ownerRef → the creator holds the bridge until it confirms the COS ownerRef is present, then drops it.

    Because the bridge is transient (dropped once a COS owns the Secret), it does not reintroduce the "long-lived owner never releases → leak across upgrades" problem. The t=1/t=2 orphan (template referenced a Secret at t=1, the COS was never created, template moved to t=2) is, by this contract, the creator's cleanup — its bridge cascades on creator deletion, or the creator reclaims it directly.

    This contract is documented here but implemented later, on the CE / operator-controller side.

    Interaction with the existing applier ownerRef patching (main)

    On main the operator-controller applier already owns referenced Secrets: at COS creation it patches an ownerRef with Controller=true (patchSecretOwnerReferences / ensureSecretOwnerReferences / patchOwnerRefsOnSecrets in internal/operator-controller/applier/boxcutter.go). Secret names are per-revision there (<cosName>-<hash>), so each Secret has exactly one owner and Controller=true is correct and non-conflicting today.

    This PR does not modify the applier (it is CE / operator-controller side, explicitly deferred). The two mechanisms coexist cleanly:

    • For an operator-controller-created COS, the applier patches the ownerRef at creation (before the COS controller first reconciles). The COS controller's claim dedups by owner UID, sees the ref already present, and is a no-op. The flag stays Controller=true, which is fine on main (single owner per per-revision Secret).
    • For a standalone COD/COS, no applier runs, so the COS controller's claim is the sole and authoritative mechanism — adding a Controller=false ownerRef.

    Consequence (intended): the non-controller / shared-content multi-owner model only fully takes effect once the deferred CE-side rework converts the applier's patching to the transient non-controller bridge (and stops setting Controller=true). Until then the headline multi-owner behavior is exercised and tested via the standalone path; the operator-controller path keeps its current main behavior unchanged. This is deliberate, and matches the "CE later" scope.

    No orphan-window regression: the operator-controller path keeps at-creation applier ownership; the standalone path's pre-COS window is the creator's responsibility by contract (for a bare COS, the creator is the user).

    Adoption

    Adoption needs no special handling because ownership rides on the COS, independent of creation order:

    • COS before COD, matching template: the COS already owns its Secrets; the COD adopts the COS as its active revision; nothing to reconcile for Secrets.
    • COS before COD, new template same group: the COD creates a new revision (which owns the new Secrets) and the pre-existing COS becomes an archived revision (still owning its Secrets until pruned, then cascaded).

    There is no group-label discovery, no sweeper race, and no grace window to tune.

    What the object-controller implements

    1. COS controller — claim referenced Secrets. For each referenced Secret in the COS's own spec phases, get the Secret and ensure a non-controller ownerRef pointing at the COS — regardless of the Secret's type. Make it idempotent and crash-recoverable (same intent as main's ensureSecretOwnerReferences): a COS that failed to patch on a prior reconcile re-attempts on the next, deduping by owner UID.
    2. Discovery is ref-driven, not label-driven. Do not reuse main's label-list discovery (List(InNamespace(SystemNamespace), MatchingLabels{RevisionNameKey: cos.Name})): a standalone COS may reference Secrets in arbitrary namespaces that carry no revision-name label, so ownership must be driven off the COS's own refs. (Builder-check Q3.)
    3. Claim on existence, independent of content validity. A referenced Secret that exists but fails a content precondition (mutable, malformed, changed) is still claimed, so it is still reclaimed when the COS is deleted. Wire the claim so it is not gated behind the content short-circuits — ownership tracks lifecycle, not content health; the preconditions are reported separately. (Builder-check Q4.)
    4. No type gate. This design does not restrict which Secret types may be referenced (see Future work), so the claim owns every referenced Secret.
    5. COS controller — precondition reporting unchanged. Keep the existing Ready=False/PreconditionsNotMet behavior for missing / mutable / changed / malformed referenced content.
    6. COD controller — no Secret logic. Confirm pruning an archived COS cascades its Secrets (it does, via COS ownership). Keep validateTemplateMetadata. Optionally propagate the latest revision's Ready reason/message onto the COD condition.
    7. RBAC. The object-controller needs Secret get/list/update in the namespaces it resolves refs from. It does not need Secret delete (cascade handles deletion).
    8. Docs. Record the creator contract where COD/COS behavior is documented.

    Testing approach

    • Unit (COS claim): ensures a non-controller ownerRef is added to each referenced Secret regardless of type; is idempotent; additive-only + UID-dedup (no-op when an ownerRef with the COS's UID already exists); re-adds after a simulated prior failure; claims on existence independent of content validity; skips a not-yet-created Secret; claims a referenced Secret once even when multiple objects reference it.
    • Unit (COD): pruning an archived revision does not require any Secret-level action; validateTemplateMetadata unchanged.
    • envtest (ownerRef write, not cascade): envtest runs only kube-apiserver
    • etcd — not kube-controller-manager — so the garbage collector does not run there and cascade deletion cannot be asserted in envtest. Use envtest only to validate the write against a real API server:
    • The claim patch is accepted on an immutable Secret (confirms metadata is patchable despite immutability) and is idempotent across a real Get/Patch roundtrip.
    • Coexistence: a Secret already carrying an applier-style Controller=true ownerRef is not double-ref'd by the claim (UID-dedup no-op).
    • GC cascade is an e2e concern (see below) because only a real cluster runs the garbage collector.

    Adoption needs no dedicated secret test: with COS-only ownership and no sweeper, nothing deletes Secrets except k8s GC on owner deletion, so there is no premature-deletion path for adoption to trigger. Ownership simply rides on the COS.

    E2E (standalone COD/COS)

    These are the end-to-end, user-observable scenarios for this PR. They live in the experimental suites (@BoxcutterRuntime) alongside the existing revision.feature / cos_*.feature / cod_*.feature, which already create and reference object-data Secrets. They exercise the standalone path — a COD/COS driven directly, with no ClusterExtension/applier involved — which is where this PR's new behavior is actually observable (the operator-controller path is unchanged; see "Interaction with the existing applier").

    1. Standalone COS cleanup. Create an object-data Secret and a COS that references it; the COS becomes Ready. Confirm the Secret carries a (non-controller) ownerRef to the COS. Delete the COS → the referenced object-data Secret is eventually removed (cascade).
    2. Standalone COD cleanup. Create a COD whose template references object-data Secret content; it rolls out a revision (COS). Delete the COD → its COS revision(s) and their object-data Secrets are eventually removed.
    3. Type-agnostic adoption + GC. Create a COS that references an immutable, non-object-data (e.g. Opaque) Secret holding a manifest; the COS becomes Ready and the Secret gains a non-controller ownerRef to the COS. Delete the COS → the Secret is eventually removed. This pins the "any referenced Secret is adopted and garbage-collected" contract (and documents that referencing a Secret hands its lifecycle to the COS, pending the future type restriction).

    Regression guard (existing, not new). uninstall.feature's "Removing ClusterExtension cascades to grouped revisions and their content Secrets" covers the CE → COS → Secret cascade. This PR is additive (applier untouched), so that scenario must continue to pass; name it as a guard rather than modifying it.

    Step reuse: the suites already have steps for applying a COS/COD, waiting on Ready, creating object-data Secrets, and asserting a resource "is installed" / "is eventually not found". Scenarios 1–3 should compose from those plus (if not present) a step asserting a Secret's ownerRef points at a given COS and a step asserting a Secret still exists after a deletion.

    What's left to the builder

    • The precise call site in the COS controller for the claim step, and whether to reuse/relocate the existing main ownerRef helpers (patchOwnerRefsOnSecrets, ensureSecretOwnerReferences) from the CE-side applier into an object-controller-owned helper. (Today those live in internal/operator-controller/applier/boxcutter.go and set Controller=true; the object-controller version must set Controller=false.)
    • Whether to resolve a ref's default namespace (empty → OLM system namespace) before claiming, matching how resolveObjectRef resolves it.
    • Whether, and exactly how, to propagate the latest revision's Ready reason/message onto the COD condition (the optional legibility improvement).
    • Exact RBAC marker changes and manifest regeneration.
